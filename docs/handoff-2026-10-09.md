## 2. The synthetic corruptions run as coded but are mis-calibrated

**Question asked:** are fog, glare, low-light and the other filters applied correctly, at the
right intensity? Nothing was scored; only images were regenerated and measured.

**Method.** `scripts/inspect_corruptions.py` regenerates frames exactly as the cache builder does:
`make_corruption(name, severity, seed)` from `src/uqfusion/eval/corruptions.py` on
`cv2.imread(path)`, with the frame's index in `runs/derived/paired_val_vis.txt`. VIS draw 941
(the Phase 3 development draw), IR draw 911. Statistics are over 150 day and 80 night paired
frames (fixed sample, seed 0), on the image rows of the letterboxed canvas unless marked pad.
Albumentations 2.0.8, the version `requirements.txt` pins. Contact sheets: `vis_sheet_A.png`
(fog, low-light), `vis_sheet_B.png` (glare, rain, blur, noise), `ir_sheet.png`; numbers in
`stats.json`.

**The plumbing is right:** the severity mapping, per-frame seeding (`seed * 100003 + index`) and
library version match what built the caches. The problems are in what the filters do.

| Condition | What it actually does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-light** (s2 in the benchmark) | Additive brightness subtraction with clipping (`RandomBrightnessContrast`, brightness −0.5 to −0.7 of 255 at s2). Day image mean 109.8 → 21.0 / 5.5 / 0.5 at s1/s2/s3, with 58% / 82% / 97% of pixels at black. Real night, pohang01 clean: mean 7.9, 32% black. On night frames s2 leaves 99.9% black. | **Too strong at every severity, and the wrong model.** s2 day is darker than real night. Dark hulls on dark water are erased first. Real low light scales brightness (and adds noise); it does not clip. |
| **Fog** | Albumentations 2.0.8 ends `RandomFog` with a fixed Gaussian blur of size min(h, w)//30 = 21 px. Fine detail (Laplacian variance of the image rows) falls to 1.5% / 1.3% / 1.1% of clean at s1/s2/s3, against 9.6% for motion blur s3. Severity only changes a veil of overlapping white discs: mean +18 / +43 / +68. On night frames fog lifts the mean from 7.9 to 43 / 88 / 133. | **Mostly blur, not haze.** The hard part does not change with severity, the texture is blotchy, and night fog is a bright veil, which is physically backwards. |
| **Glare** | `RandomSunFlare`'s default source region is the top half of the 640×640 canvas, rows 0–319, and the top pad is rows 0–150. The source lands in the pad on 45% of frames (279 sampled), at every severity. Severity only grows the core (`src_radius` 120/200/320); the ghost circles are the same, so the mean rises +13 / +14 / +18. A sun flare is also applied to night frames. | **Weak, flat across severities, and often off-image.** |
| Rain s2 | Long grey streaks over image and pad, mean ×0.8. | Plausible as a synthetic effect. |
| Blur s3 | Motion blur, kernel 15–25 px. | Plausible as "heavy". |
| Noise s2 | σ 0.1–0.2 of 255 (25–51 grey levels), per channel. On night frames clipping at 0 rectifies it: mean 7.9 → 19.9. | Heavy, and not zero-mean at night. |
| **IR fog, glare** (s2) | The same visible-light filters on thermal frames. Glare paints coloured lens-flare circles on a grayscale image; per-channel noise adds false colour too. | **Not physically motivated.** LWIR cameras do not flare in colour, and fog attenuates LWIR differently from visible light. |

**Applied after letterboxing.** Every filter runs on the 640×640 canvas, so the grey pad (114)
is corrupted too: fog lifts its mean to 136 at s2, low-light sets it to 0, rain and noise alter
86–99% of pad pixels. The **gate statistics are unaffected**: `frame_brightness.py` and
`frame_structure.py` read the content rows only. The detector does see the altered pad.

**What this touches in the paper** (`PAPER_DRAFT2.md`, and the venue drafts built from it):

* §6.3, §6.6 and §9 item 2 say the night rule is right on fogged and low-light night. True, but
  nearly trivially: those frames are blacked out (low-light) or blurred to about 1% of their
  fine detail (fog). Figures 9–10 display these very frames.
* §6.7, Table 6: the IR night rule's false-night rate under IR fog (43.8 / 86.7 / 94.8%) and IR
  glare (19–27%) is measured with visible-light filters applied to thermal frames, not with
  anything thermal fog or sun does.
* Fog-cell results measure robustness to a 21-px blur plus a bright veil more than to haze. The
  veil veto (`fit_veil_gate.py`) is a Laplacian-variance bound fitted on clean frames; it catches
  synthetic fog because of the fixed blur, so its fog performance says little about real fog.
* The pohang04 look scored corrupted cells too (Amendment 9: draws VIS 941–944, IR 951–954).
  pohang04 is spent, so a rebuilt corruption could never be re-scored there.
* The headline σ verdict is a within-cell comparison against a shuffled control, so it is
  probably robust to the corruption model. **Not re-run; an expectation, not a result.**

**The decision (Laksh's):**

1. **Disclose (recommended, cheap).** Add these measurements to §5.1 and §9, and soften the
   night-rule and §6.7 wording. A task card for this was proposed in the Claude Code app
   (`task_d49f891b`, "Disclose synthetic-corruption calibration in the paper"); it has not run.
2. **Rebuild and re-score (expensive).** Corrupt the image rows only (or the native frame before
   letterboxing); fog from the scattering model I = J·t + A(1 − t) without the fixed blur;
   low-light as exposure scaling plus sensor noise, calibrated to pohang01's real statistics;
   glare with `flare_roi` inside the image rows and a severity that changes intensity; no sun
   flare on night or IR frames. Then rebuild every corrupted development cache and re-score.
   This changes registered results, so it is a declared deviation, and it costs several GPU-days.
   Development cells only (see pohang04 above).

```
py -3.13 venues/build_long_drafts.py                       # TMLR and JOE drafts from Draft 2
PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 py -3.13 venues/build_tmlr_latex.py # venues/tmlr/main.tex, references.bib, figures/
```

Compiling TMLR needs files that are not in the repo: Tectonic 0.17.0 and `tmlr.sty`, `tmlr.bst`,
`fancyhdr.sty` from github.com/JmlrOrg/tmlr-style-file. The copies used so far lived in a session
scratchpad and should be assumed gone. Put each download in its own empty directory, compile in a
scratch copy of `venues/tmlr/` with `tectonic --keep-logs main.tex`, and copy back only `main.pdf`.
